# Mission films

A film of a mission is the art opening plus the full scripted solution (`tools/solutions/<id>.solution.mjs`), played in the real game with player orders only, captioned, with a soundtrack built from the game's own audio. The M3 film (`m03.cut.mjs`) was the first; every mission with a solution can be filmed the same way.

Videos never go into the repository (the asset budget): they live in `commandos-shots/videos/`.

## Film a mission

```sh
# from a checkout nobody else is driving (it uses the GPU for minutes); run it in the background and poll the log
node tools/solutions/film-mission.mjs m01 --sheet
#   ffmpeg: --ffmpeg <bin>, else $FFMPEG, else `ffmpeg` on the PATH, else the imageio_ffmpeg build in the uv / micromamba caches
#   (film-ffmpeg.mjs; the Playwright copy cannot encode H.264)
#   -> ../commandos-shots/videos/m01.mp4   (+ m01-sheet.jpg, work/m01/timeline.json, work/m01.capture.log, work/m01.mix.log)
```

`film-mission.mjs` runs the three steps below and checks the result; the last line is `FILM_RESULT {json}` and the exit code is 0 only when every check passed. Options: `--videos <dir>` (default `../commandos-shots/videos`, or `$FILM_VIDEOS`), `--mp4 <file>`, `--crf 26` instead of `--bitrate`, `--no-opening`, `--opening-sec 17`, `--frame-waits` (see Wait framing below), `--keep-frames` (the JPEG frames, ~220 KB each, are removed after a good mix; keep them to mix again), `--mix-only` (mix again from an existing capture whose frames were kept; `mix-video.mjs <work dir> --audio-only x.m4a` needs no frames).

The steps one by one:

```sh
node tools/solutions/capture-video.mjs m01 --out <work dir> [--no-opening] [--opening-sec 17] [--dry]   # frames + timeline.json
node tools/solutions/mix-video.mjs <work dir> <out.mp4> [--bitrate 1400k] [--audio-only out.m4a]
node tools/solutions/contact-sheet.mjs <work dir> <sheet.jpg> [--tile 320x180] [--list]                # 4x4 key frames (--tile 320x180: 1280 px wide; --list: the plan only)
```

`--dry` plays both passes without drawing and prints the film's length per checkpoint step (to tune a cut).

## What the capture does (`capture-video.mjs`)

- **Pass 1** plays the solution logic-only (~15–60 s) to learn when things happen (kills, objectives, checkpoints).
- **Art opening** (`film-opening.mjs`, at most 19.5 s, `--no-opening` to skip): the game's briefing tour (`src/ui/tour.js` `tourStops`: objectives, the densest guard cluster, the extraction, the team's start) turned round into a slow pan — a zoom-2 close-up of the first objective under the mission's title card (mission number and name, place and date, objective, "never spotted" when pass 1 had no detection), then the game's default zoom over the danger and the extraction, then the team's start at the film zoom, where the solution begins. Each stop has a short caption. A mission whose briefing tour has too few stops (M12's objectives name no building and its extraction is a car: the tour was a hop between two places) names its places itself in its cut — `openingStops(tourStops, world, def)` returns them in the order wanted (`{ kind, x, z, label, dwell }`); the planner keeps that order, drops nothing and adds no start stop, so the opening ends at the cut's last stop (`m12.cut.mjs`: the cell under the title card, the roofs, the mosque platform, the car, and then the film cuts to the courtyard where the Spy begins). It runs in a world of its own that is stepped in real time (patrols walk, water flows) and thrown away, **in a browser page of its own, closed when it is done**: a drawn world leaves state behind in the art that later loads in the same page share (the library's LOD states, which the static solids of the next load are measured from), so a drawn opening in the page of the filmed run gave pass 2 a world that was not pass 1's — M14's lorry measured 17 cm shorter, a guard's path round it one waypoint short, and the drawn run parted from the logic-only one 21 game minutes in (the lured men came back late, the Sapper was seen). The solution then starts on a fresh load in a page that has drawn nothing, as pass 1's was, so the run is exactly the scripted one.
- **Pass 2** reloads and plays the same run drawn: every tick is `Game.step(dt)`, a frame is rendered and grabbed every N ticks, N set by a speed director (4× while someone walks, 8× while only crawling, 16× while everyone waits and up to 32× once a wait has run past 16 s of film; real time around kills and blasts it knows from pass 1). The camera follows the commando last ordered (his vehicle when aboard). Stage title, step caption, game clock and speed are a DOM layer captured with the canvas.
- **Cards and captions without a cut**: title card and end card are built from the mission's briefing data and the run (`Mission complete`, objectives ticked, game time, detections, kills, commandos alive). The stage title is the walkthrough's chapter (`<id>.walkthrough.mjs`) or the solution's own stage title; the step caption is the walkthrough step's text (its first sentences while they fit 160 characters) or the checkpoint's name. A step is the stretch that ENDS at its checkpoint (`docs/walkthrough-format.md`), so its caption goes up when the step begins (the stage starts, or the checkpoint before is reached) and stays until the next one (`film-captions.mjs`); each caption is given its reading time (24 characters a second, 2.2 to 6.5 s) before the next may replace it, a caption overtaken while it waits is dropped. A cut that words most of the checkpoints (`m03.cut.mjs`: `CAPTIONS`, keyed by checkpoint, worded for what has just happened) keeps the older rule: its caption goes up at the checkpoint and stays for a while; one that words only a few (`m01.cut.mjs`: D4) mends those and leaves the rest to the walkthrough. A `tools/solutions/<id>.cut.mjs` is optional and overrides all of it (`TITLES`, `CAPTIONS`, `TITLE_CARD`, `END_CARD`) and adds the art opening's places `openingStops(tourStops, world, def)`, fixed camera `shots`, speed `windows`, `follows`, timed captions `notes(cp, objT, ev)` (`{ t0, t1, text }` in game time: the step captions only change at checkpoints, so a long stretch between two of them needs its own) a `pitchAt` (see `m03.cut.mjs`) and the switch `FRAME_WAITS` (the wait framing below). The hooks `shots`, `windows`, `follows` and `notes` get the checkpoints' times (`cp(k)`), the objectives' times and pass 1's events `ev` — kills, blasts, objectives done, and `plant` (a charge set: `{ t, kind: 'plant', what, by, x, y, z }`, the bomb's place) — so a cut can film what the generic director only passes at fast-forward. `m01.cut.mjs` is the small kind: it mends the one stretch the generic director leaves quiet (M1's raft, parked 6½ minutes of game time waiting for a gap on the north beach, shown from the beach at 40× instead) and leaves the rest to the director. `m11.cut.mjs` is the same kind: it films the Sapper's three charges in real time (the `plant` events: at 4–8× he is a dot) and, after the quarry blast the director shows, visits the yard rig, the tunnel mouth and the valley rig that the same second's charges brought down out of the picture. `m08.cut.mjs` too: M8's Sniper lies on the road for 16 minutes of game time waiting for the moment each of three elevated guards can be shot unseen, so the camera pulls out to the whole north side of the store, where the decoy draws the two walkers out again and again, at 36×; the wait for the first shot in the start yard likewise. A film should have no stretch of 5 s or more in which nothing moves: look at the contact sheet and at the sampled frames of any run of 16× or faster before it is called done. `m14.cut.mjs` is the smallest kind: the wait framing on (M14 is 46 game minutes of stealth), and one timed note at the run's only alarm (gun 4's barrel posts found dead: the title card says "never spotted", and a siren needs its reason on screen). `m16.cut.mjs` is the same kind (M16 is 20 game minutes of stealth): the wait framing, and one timed note at the run's one siren, which is by design (the fifth round kills e15 in plain view of five guards, so his fall is seen and the siren sounds as the objective is done). `m10.cut.mjs` is the smallest kind of all: the wait framing and nothing else (M10 is 21 game minutes of stealth on foot, never seen, and raises no alarm). `m19.cut.mjs` adds a time-lapse to the wait framing: the Green Beret's two long waits for the base's lorry to stand by the post e27 (E4 to F1, 326 game seconds in the hollow below the conveyor switch, and H1 to H2, 146 in the gap behind the drums) run at 48× — at 16× to 30× the camera stayed on the yard for 10 s and 5 s with little moving in it — the first one captioned. `m20.cut.mjs` is of the smallest kind again: the wait framing (M20 is 97 game minutes of stealth, never seen; its one siren is the charge itself, which the walkthrough's step text already explains); its header lists five waits in which the picture stays still at 16× to 32× and which the film has time-lapsed after the capture. `m09.cut.mjs` is of the smallest kind again: the wait framing (M09 is 13 game minutes of stealth on foot, never seen; its one siren is the first blast, which the walkthrough's step text already explains); its film has no still fast-forward stretch. `m18.cut.mjs` is the cut of the solution lane (stage titles, a caption for every checkpoint, the cards, fixed shots at the span and the lorry's drives, real-time windows at the dive, the harpoons and the blast) plus the wait framing (M18 is 28 game minutes of stealth, the three kills the Marine's harpoon, its only alarm the bridge going up); its header lists three still stretches of stage B (the first pair edging west, crawling the south edge and waiting in the lorry's lee: a dark field with one man in it at 8× to 16×) which the film has time-lapsed after the capture. `m04.cut.mjs` is of the smallest kind again: the wait framing (M04 is 35 game minutes of stealth on foot, never seen; its one siren is the headquarters' blast, which the walkthrough's step text already explains). Its film is the one that found the unpaced stretch (next bullet).
- **The ground under the camera** (`film-camera.mjs`): the view is centred on a point at the height of the ground there. `world.groundY` is the terrain relief alone — 0 on a deck such as M5's summit (16 m) or M3's dam crest (7.3 m) — which put the view `y·cot(pitch)` (19 m on the summit) short of the barracks the film was about to blow up and of the opening's first stop. `standHeight(world, x, z)` takes the highest of the relief and the game's walking surface (`grid.surfaceY`) at the point and on rings round it (a structure's footprint has no walking surface); the opening's pan, the blast shots and any cut shot without a `y` use it (a man or a vehicle carries his own `y`).
- **Unpaced stretches** (driver `unpaced`): a solution that steps the world from two flows of its own at once (M4's bridgehead strike: the Sniper's third shot while the Green Beret and the Marine crawl out) fixes the order the flows tick in by the promise hops each takes between two ticks, which the logic-only pass's step (nothing returned) decides. The drawn pass's step is async and waits for a frame, so the other flow ticks on meanwhile and the order changes: M4's drawn run parted from the logic-only one there (checkpoints 0.15 s apart, the post-blast run to the boat, tuned to the exact timeline, then seen; `--trace` named the tick, the Sniper's order one tick early). A freeze while a frame is drawn does not mend it (the flows' phases differ and a release resets them), so pass 2 ticks such a stretch at once and draws no frame inside it, as the in-game replay does (`solution-replay.js` `_step`), and ends the Sniper's scope show the stretch left stale. A solution marks the stretch as short as it can (M4 clears `D.unpaced` when the third shot is down: the runners are the only flow left): the film jumps over about two seconds of game time there. `capture-video.mjs mNN --trace --no-shot` checks any film in a few minutes: `identical every tick`.
- **Wait framing** (`--frame-waits` on `capture-video.mjs` / `film-mission.mjs`, or `export const FRAME_WAITS = true` in a mission's cut; `film-camera.mjs` `waitFrame`): in a stealth mission most of the film is waiting — the speed director runs at 16× and more while everyone waits, and the camera stays on the man who lies still, so the picture is a still one (the guards he waits for pacing at its edge) for 5 to 8 s at a time. With it, once everyone has waited 1.5 s of film, the camera shows the waiting man together with the guards the walkthrough step names (its `look` / `cones` tags: the ones whose eyes the wait is about; else the three nearest walkers within 30 m of him), each by his whole beat (the ends of his route), so the framing sits still while they pace in it; a patrol whose route is longer than 36 m is not chased at 16× (it counts, where it stands, only when the step names it). It is the game's own always-action rule for its walkthrough (`debug/walkthrough.js` `_liveShot`, the user's standing request of 2026-10-15 "Also no long quiet periods, always action in the walkthrough") with the walkthrough camera's framing maths (`framePoints`: the man first, the farthest guards dropped when all do not fit; never closer than zoom 0.8, never wider than 0.45). Off by default, so the existing films stay as they were; the cuts of M9, M10, M13, M14, M15, M16, M18, M19 and M20 turn it on. A cut's `shots` still win over it.
- **The Sniper's scope** (the user's standing request for the walkthrough, 2026-10-22: "we should see the sniper scope moving around before sniper hits target"): when the squad carries the sniper rifle, the game's own scope show (`src/debug/walkthrough-scope.js` `ScopeShow`) runs on the film's clock — at the Sniper's order the scope comes up off the man, searches round him, finds him and settles with its sway; the film slows (down to 0.15×, with frames between ticks) to the pace that lands the shot at the end of the show, the recoil, the scope comes down. The camera stays on the man in the glass; the run is untouched.
- **The cue sheet**: the game's own audio handlers (`src/audio/event-map.js` `installHandlers`) listen to the filmed run through a recorder in place of the audio engine, so `timeline.json` carries what the game would have played — one-shots `{t, id, x, z, g, rate}`, loops, automatic-fire bursts, bomb ticks, the siren — plus the threat log the score follows (enemy states, deaths, the alarm) and each frame's camera (`cx, cz, vw`), the listener of the game's distance model.
- The result checks that both passes won and that their checkpoints are identical (drawing did not change the run).
- **When the drawn run parts from the logic-only one** (the checkpoints differ, or pass 2 loses what pass 1 won): `capture-video.mjs <id> --out <dir> --trace [--no-shot] [--max-frames N] [--trace-dump a:b]` hashes every tick of both passes (`src/debug/sim-trace.js`, the cold-link tool's recorder) and prints the first tick at which they differ and the units there (`--trace-dump 77716:77724` prints those ticks' state lines from both passes: a path of 8 waypoints against 7 names a different nav grid or clearance); `--no-shot` draws the frames without saving them (4× faster, no JPEGs) for such a hunt. Each capture also fingerprints the two passes' worlds at tick 0 (`src/debug/gameplay-fingerprint.js`: every nav-grid layer, the body solids, links…) and writes `worlds` into the timeline; `film-mission.mjs` prints a NOTE when they differ — a drawn run that is not the one pass 1 played is then a matter of time, however well the first checkpoints match.

## What the mix does (`mix-video.mjs`, `film-audio.mjs`, `film-mixer.mjs`)

Nothing is written for one mission; everything comes from the mission's data and the game's tables.

- **Music**: the mission's start stinger (`startCueFor`: Norway `start_1`, North Africa `start_2`, Normandy `start_3`, the Rhine `start_4`, Final Assault `start_5`, covert `start_6`), then the tension chain exactly as `MusicDirector` chains it (each segment from its own loop start at the previous one's body end, never repeating the one before), the alert layer (`mission_alert`) crossfaded in on a bar line while the game's `ThreatTracker` says guards are in combat or searching or the alarm runs and out after the calm hold, and the success cue at the win (`fail_*` for a lost run) with the score fading over a second.
- **Ambience**: the theatre's beds from `ambienceFor(def)` — snow and fjord wind, desert wind, surf, birds, crickets, town, the M2/M3/M19 rivers — at the game's gains scaled to the film's level and capped by each recording's loudness (no bed over −30 LUFS); positional layers (M3's waterfall) follow the camera and end when their structure is destroyed; sparse far barks.
- **Effects**: every cue of the sheet with the game's `SFX` gain and `spatialize` distance model from the view centre (culled when out of range, panned by screen side, far blasts as the distant recording), the game's recorded takes where it has them and its own synth recipes (`src/audio/synth.js`) where it has not (harpoon, raft hiss, decoy beep, trap snap…), de-duplicated like the game and limited to three of a kind per half second; footsteps, crawling and the ducks and fish only in the real-time stretches (at 16× they would rattle); one recording is never played twice at once; bomb ticks 2 Hz → 4 Hz; the siren fading over 25 s.
- The sum is made sample by sample in `film-mixer.mjs` (a film has thousands of effects: too many inputs for one ffmpeg graph), peak-limited (a shot is ten dB over the score) and loudness-normalised to −16 LUFS / −2 dBTP — one linear gain when the peaks allow it, the dynamic `loudnorm` of the M3 film when not — then limited again; video is x264 (`--crf`, or two-pass `--bitrate`) with AAC audio. The AAC bitrate is checked first (`film-aac.mjs`): the native encoder now and then puts a burst of full-scale samples on one channel at a sharp transient (M16's film: the Sniper's bolt click, +0.6 dBFS in a soundtrack that peaks at -5.5 dBFS before the encoder), at one bitrate and not at the next (192k and 256k, not 160k or 224k), so the soundtrack is encoded alone, decoded and measured, and a bitrate whose decode peaks over -1 dBFS is passed over for the next (`--abitrate` is the first tried; `--no-aac-check` skips the trial). Without it the film's `audible, not clipping` check fails with a peak over 0 dBFS.
- An older timeline without the cue sheet (the October M3 captures) still mixes: its music and ambience come from the mission definition, with no effects.
