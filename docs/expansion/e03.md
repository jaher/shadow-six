**Zirkus Adria winter quarters, Dolní Lhota on the Elbe lowland, Bohemia (fictional circus and village, real river) · 16 April 1944, 09:30 · Bright spring morning on a requisitioned circus ground · `temperate` · 150 × 120 m · difficulty 3 of 8**

Campaign *Lights Out* (`BCD`). Conventions as e01. No guest. File `src/missions/expansion/e03_winter_quarters.js`; registration: `title 'Winter Quarters'`, `place 'Dolní Lhota, Bohemia'`, `date '1944-04-16'`, `pin { lat: 50.20, lon: 15.30 }`.

## 0. Provenance and changes

The previous e03 (*The Keeper's Coat*: a Balkan lakeside estate with a private menagerie, three lions in a sunken pit, four ostriches behind a pen gate, a zookeeper whose coat was the Spy's uniform, a chief who fled in the staff car on alarm) was flagged as the 1999 zoo mission with a different owner. **Setting, layout and objective script are replaced.** Kept: the slot, the arc beat (Ansbach seen in person with two Gestapo and untouchable: the campaign's first Gestapo contact), the first puppet, the animal mechanics the catalogue requires (lion, ostrich, pen gate, keeper type, chicken, kennel dogs released on alarm), the lush spring light.

- **A circus's winter quarters, not a zoo or an estate.** The animals are a circus's: the lions rehearse in the **ring** under a half-raised big top (a kerb, not a pit); the ostriches are performing birds in a paddock; the `zookeeper` type is the **animal trainer**; the circus people are kept on under requisition to feed the beasts and play for the troops. *Referat N*'s Protectorate intercept station sits in the **riding hall** because it has a high roof and open ground for masts.
- **No staff car, no flight.** The quiet-or-lose rule is the **well**: on alarm the station chief drops the key-table binder down the camp well.
- **The keeper's coat is not the core trick.** The Spy's taught disguise is a private's tunic from a sentry (the hanger, taught in e02); the trainer's grey coat is an optional second uniform that passes the patrol's sergeant.
- **A snitch outside a prison**: the circus's **manager** is an informer who walks the lanes and reports to the nearest sentry. The `snitch` type is taught here, in civilian clothes, and reused in e08.
- **Bohemia, not the Balkans.** The river is the Elbe; the way out is a **rope ferry**.
- Count 28/33, unchanged. Par **1080/1560** (Hard/Easy), set from the reference solution's wins at about 1049 s and 1511 s (round 1 of the verifier: the design's 780/860 was well under what the trainer's round and the chief's walks to the villa allow).

## 2. Briefing (our own words)

**Historical.** "April 1944. *Referat N* has moved a station into the Protectorate to listen for the Czech and Polish sets that London and Moscow are dropping into the hills. They wanted a tall roof far from any town and found one: the riding hall of a circus in its winter quarters on the Elbe meadows. The circus people stay on under requisition; they feed the lions and play for the garrison on Sundays. And this week the head of *Referat N*, Oberst Viktor Ansbach, is there on inspection, lodged in the owner's villa."

**Colonel.** "Four of you. The station's key-table binder lies on the chief's table in the riding hall; the chief, a Leutnant Brandt, crosses to the villa every few minutes to report. Bring the binder out, then drop the main mast with a charge at its foot: the hall is off the air for a season. The circus ground is full of eyes that are not soldiers: a trainer who runs for the barracks if he sees you, a manager who sells what he sees. The lions are loose in the ring at this hour; walk round it. Keep three paces from the birds. You leave by the rope ferry at the towpath. One thing more. Ansbach takes his coffee on the villa's veranda with two men in black beside him. They see through any coat. **You will see him. You will not touch him.** Dead, his index goes to Berlin and two hundred people hang; alive, next year, he is ours."

**Notebook hints.**
- The trainer stands at the ring kerb with his back to the camp while the lions work. If he sees you, he shouts and runs. Chloroform first.
- The manager in the bowler hat is an informer. He walks to the nearest sentry with whatever he has seen. He is a civilian: the fist works.
- A handcuffed man can be worked like a puppet (R) from within pistol range, as long as you can see him and nobody can see you. A sergeant on strings can talk to privates and patrols.
- If the hall's alarm sounds, the chief takes the binder to the well. Take the binder first; drop the mast last.
- The dogs are kennelled at the gate until the alarm. Then they are not.
