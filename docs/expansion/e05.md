## 0. Provenance and changes

The previous e05 kept the fort but ran a **prison break**: a cell block, six prisoners who ran for a boat when the bridge came down, a snitch in an exercise yard, a jailed guest, an officer's tunic on a line, and a cutter out through a mine gap. The review flagged that script as the 1999 POW-camp, jailed-commando and courier profiles stacked. **The fort stays; the objective script is new.** Kept: the slot, the island star fort in a wet moat at dusk in a gale, the hoist as the covert entry (the lift, taught in e04, reused here), the puppet as the core, the Gestapo as the fort's masters, the index, Grethe's rescue.

- **No prison.** Hammerø is *Referat N*'s **Baltic intercept and direction-finding station**: the signal mast on the NE bastion, a **radio casemate** against the east rampart whose iron door opens only to the garrison, the index in the commandant's study, and the Gestapo's regional office in the old powder magazine, where **Grethe is being questioned**. She is not in a cell: she joins when a commando enters the office.
- **No snitch, no prisoners' run, no mines, no cutter.** The team leaves as it came: down the hoist and along the shore to the folboats in the cove.
- **The drawbridge is down at start and raised against the garrison**: the fort's company lives in a hut camp on the glacis across the moat; with the bridge up and its keeper bound, forty men stand on the wrong side of their own moat.
- **The officer's tunic comes off the adjutant** with the hanger (e02's lesson), not off a line. **Grethe's lure is "small talk"**, not a lipstick (§5, campaign.md §5).
- Count 30/37, par 960/1050, unchanged.

## 2. Briefing (our own words)

**Historical.** "October 1944. The Germans have disbanded the Danish police and the Gestapo have the country to themselves. *Referat N*'s Baltic station sits in an old star fort on a granite islet off Bornholm: a mast on the north-east bastion, receivers in a casemate, and in the commandant's study the card index of two hundred names that the station's bearings have produced. Three days ago the Gestapo arrested the woman who typed most of those cards."

**Colonel.** "Grethe Holm, code name Lark, has worked the switchboard of *Referat N*'s Copenhagen office since 1942 and passed us every name that crossed it. They know that now. She is on Hammerø, in the Gestapo office in the fort, being questioned by a Kriminalrat who has not yet had her shot because he wants the rest of her friends. Three of you land on the islet at dusk. The fort's garrison lives in a hut camp on the glacis and walks in and out over a drawbridge; inside the walls at night there are only the Gestapo, the signals people and the gate. Go in by the ammunition hoist from the harbour magazine. Get Grethe out, take the index, take the station's call-sign book from the radio casemate: its door opens only to their own men, so handcuff one and work him like a puppet. Then raise the bridge from the switch room by the gate and leave the way you came: the camp stays outside its own fort until morning." (As built: the switch room by the gate, §16.)

**Notebook hints.**
- The hoist carries four, or one carried man and two. It creaks: keep the nearest sentry well off.
- The Gestapo wear black. They see through every uniform and they do not stop to talk. They can be knocked out from behind like anyone else.
- A puppet opens doors his own side keeps locked and works switches. Keep him within pistol range and in sight, and keep yourself unseen while you hold the strings.
- Grethe can stop a soldier with a question and keep his eyes on her while she talks. Not a Gestapo man.
- The bridge keeper lowers the bridge again the moment he can. See to him.
- If the fort's alarm sounds, the Kriminalrat takes the index to the signal station and burns it.
