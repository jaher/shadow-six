# e07 · *White Out* (FINAL design, revised after the originality review)

**Hochjoch pass, on the Austrian–Italian border, Ötztal Alps (fictional pass works, real range) · 23 March 1945, 10:40 · High snow under a hard blue sky · `snow` · 120 × 200 m · difficulty 7 of 8**

Campaign *Lights Out* (`BCD`). Conventions as e01. No guest. File `src/missions/expansion/e07_white_out.js`; registration: `title 'White Out'`, `place 'Hochjoch pass, Ötztal Alps, Austria'`, `date '1945-03-23'`, `pin { lat: 46.80, lon: 10.82 }`.

## 0. Provenance and changes

The setting (the pass, the avalanche gallery under a loaded cornice, the cable car to the ridge, the snow glare) was judged original and is kept whole. Two things in the objective script were flagged as 1999 mission scripts: **the named officer who fled in a Kübelwagen on alarm, or on foot if the car was blocked**, and **the empty-handed start with knapsacks to recover as the opening objective**. Both are gone.

- **The team starts with its kit.** Only the heavy container went astray in the drop: it holds the **Sapper's demolition pack** and the **Driver's rifle**. Two `knapsack`s on the watched snowfield, a mid-mission objective, not a disarmed opening.
- **Major Vering does not run.** He is at his desk in the hotel and is taken there, quietly, by the Spy: `deliver` to the cable car. No car, no foot chase, no road block.
- **The quiet-or-lose rule is the press**: the convoy's middle lorry carries the machine that prints *Referat N*'s key tables, and its Feldwebel has orders to **smash the type plates** at the first alarm. Take the plates first.
- The bowser remains the alternative way to close the pass (a pushed tank as a block, taught here; reused as cover in e08). Count 36/43, par 1080/1180, unchanged.

## 2. Briefing (our own words)

**Historical.** "March 1945. What is left of *Referat N* is going into the mountains. Major Konrad Vering, Ansbach's deputy, has the last direction-finding lorry and the machine that prints their key tables in a three-lorry convoy over the Hochjoch pass. The pass is held by mountain troops, and the convoy has stopped at the hotel on the summit for fuel and a night's sleep."

**Colonel.** "Five of you dropped last night in a storm and landed in one piece, which is more than can be said for the heavy container: it came down on the snowfield east of the road with the Sapper's charges and the Driver's rifle in it. Find it. Then close the pass: the north road runs through an avalanche gallery under a loaded cornice, and a charge in the right place brings the mountain down on it; if you prefer, the convoy's fuel bowser wedged in the tunnel and set alight does the same job, louder. The press is in the middle lorry; its type plates are the only copies of April's keys, and the Feldwebel in charge has orders to smash them at the first alarm. Take them first. Vering is at the hotel. Alive, please. The goods cable car behind the hotel runs to a station on the ridge; from there it is downhill into Italy and our lines. The summit observation post has a flak gun and a view of the north road; the ski patrol has a view of everything."

**Notebook hints.**
- Snow holds tracks. So does the mud in the hotel yard. The ski patrol follows both: walk where they walk, or where nobody walks.
- The Driver's rifle kills at thirty paces. On an open slope there is often nobody within twenty paces to hear it. Count them before you fire.
- The bowser can be pushed. A pushed bowser is a wall on wheels, and three bullets make it a bomb.
- If the hotel's alarm sounds, the Feldwebel at the press lorry smashes the plates. Thirty seconds.
- A cable car in the air is a target for the flak on the ridge. Quiet on the summit, or dead on the ridge.
